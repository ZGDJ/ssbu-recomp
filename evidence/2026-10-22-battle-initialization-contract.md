## Lifecycle and mode limits

The owner seed and vector are guest simulation state. A successful rematch handshake supplies a new agreed seed; original construction/reset must establish that match's state. Snapshot restoration must restore the selected owner, vector, counters and continuation together, without reapplying session seeding. Host handshake state is session/coordinator state, not a substitute for restored simulation state. Existing resource, membership/publication and continuation gates remain unresolved.

B found a concrete hazard for a proposed override: `rbn_match_reset` does not clear `g_hs_seed`. A nonzero `rbn_match_seed()` is therefore not proof of agreement for the current setup. Any future input override must bind to current successful setup, excluding bypass/fallback paths; this is not evidence that the existing original owner copy is stale.

The fighter diagnostic registration is process-scoped rather than a proved per-match lifecycle. The current frame-0..3 observer admission cannot automatically certify later rematches. Use fresh processes for the first paired seed experiment; rematch observation needs explicit interval/identity coverage before acceptance.

Successful room-code handshake, loop mode, failed-handshake fallback, ordinary local mode and Local Wireless are distinct paths. Loop seed access does not execute `hs_apply`; Local Wireless and ordinary local bypasses deliberately retain the game's path. A passing loop/offline trial cannot qualify actual handshake initialization.

## Engineering decision and next experiments

**Decision:** preserve the inherited runtime and original reference. Do not patch a conditional input field, override a later owner, or relocate network synchronization on the strength of offline evidence. The bounded missing fact is consistency of original selector/owner/reset outputs after an actual successful handshake, including relevant branch inputs and intervening initialization. No additional menu-wait consumer census or pre-wait observer trial resolves that fact.

At network readiness, use the existing actual host/join path with explicit `SSBU_RB_NET_SEED`, first seed 1 then a distinct nonzero seed, symmetric rules/fresh identical saves and different pre-match histories. Start with fresh processes, identified frozen packages, matching modules and separate peer bundles. Existing handshake logs and selector/constructor/reset/early observers provide the first endpoint comparison; keep menu observer off. Record handshake success/role/seed and selector branch inputs rather than inferring success from a connection. No current test was run.

- Equal selector-entry state and owner/reset outputs across histories: retain current synchronization; isolate the first-fighter rewrite with existing `SSBU_DET_OFF` bit 1 before considering its removal. Preserve polling behavior separately. Repeated uninstrumented runs then test whether observation hid instability.
- Unequal selector-entry state after equal agreed seed: restrict the next audit to handshake-to-selector initialization and branch inputs. Moving the write to a later owner is still rejected because it cannot repair earlier choices.
- Equal entry inputs/state but unequal owner/reset results: investigate the selector branch/concurrent writer or owner transfer interval. A direct-input candidate requires ordinary and selected-record cases, multiple seed values and a declared global-draw policy; do not broaden to historical menu consumers.
- Equal initialization but later telemetry divergence: move investigation to the first-fighter override or subsequent simulation; do not add another seed reset.

Every arm needs fatal/process, sealed complete interval, identity and original-reference results kept separately. A deliberate change to seed/initialization may fail the historical reference; retain that failure. A new fixture requires justification against original initialization semantics and an uninterrupted original-behavior oracle, not promotion of a mismatching output. Matching selected RNG/telemetry does not prove complete-state equality.
