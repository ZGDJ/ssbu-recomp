## Decision and invariant

Initial ring metadata, learned validation and ready/count publication must occur before `save` releases its successful hold. In the inherited chain, `save` unparks internally, then `r1_ring_capture` calls `release` (backing cleanup only), validates learned tracking and publishes. Membership can grow from 176 to 177 in that window. Moving `release` would not fix it. The decisive fixture injects exactly that membership change at unpark; acceptance requires publication at 176, then release, and refusal without published readiness on every injected failure. Restoring early release must fail the oracle at O1 and O3.

`rg_capture_base` requests a synchronous held completion only for the ring's initial save. `save_finish_held` calls `rg_base_publish` before unpark; failed mark setup, command-buffer staging or base preparation still unpark once and release backing. Early save failures retain their existing cleanup. `rg_base_publish` validates learned tracking, initializes the ring and reserves slot metadata, validates tracking again after preparation, captures metadata and only then publishes ready/count and RA cycle readiness. The second validation refuses a sticky learned error appearing during preparation. It does not establish universal writer quiescence.

Caller trace: `r1_ring_capture` uses the request; `ra_kick` initial save and legacy diagnostic full/fast-check saves leave it unset and retain ordinary save behavior. `os_snapshot` and diagnostic reporting remain after unpark. Ring lazy symbol lookup (`rg_cb_find`) and copy-worker startup (`cp_init`, including startup-failure joins) are warmed before the hold. Held ring allocation uses the existing mark-setup initialization policy (CRT allocation and VirtualAlloc); this is not a proof that every host allocator/loader writer is quiescent. No new thread startup or symbol lookup is introduced under the hold. Historical protection=177 and excluded-writer concerns remain distinct.
